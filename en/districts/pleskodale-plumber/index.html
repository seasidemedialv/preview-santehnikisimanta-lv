A plumber in Pleskodāle mostly works in low-rise houses: the neighbourhood is dominated by buildings of 1–3 storeys, from one-and-a-half-storey standard detached houses to two-storey workers' houses.

Entrance from a shared corridor, one toilet for everyone

Pleskodāle has small houses from the end of the 19th century and standard-type two-storey blocks of flats, built for the workers of the outlying factories. In them the flats are entered from a shared corridor, and the sanitary unit was originally one for everyone.

In such a building the water supply and the drains are a shared part of all the flats. If a flat needs its own sink, shower or toilet, the new outlets have to join the building’s shared drain, and the fall from the flat to it must be sufficient. Joining the shared system usually has to be agreed with the other owners or the building manager, unless the building’s documents say otherwise.

Your own shower and toilet in an old workers' house

This is the work that helps the oldest houses in Pleskodāle most: moving plumbing outlets during a renovation, before the walls are closed. First find out where the building’s shared drain pipe runs and how low it lies in relation to the floor of the new bathroom.

  1. Drain position Find the shared drain and its height. It decides whether the shower can be at floor level or needs a raised tray.
  2. Toilet outlet The Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten (not a norm in Latvia) allow the toilet outlet to be joined straight to the riser. The drains of other fixtures are joined lower down on a branch at an angle of up to 45°. This makes it harder for backflow from the toilet to reach other drains.
  3. Water supplies Each fixture gets its own connection point with a way to shut it off, and only then is the finish put on.

If the flat already has an old drain pipe to the shared line, it can be replaced separately, see waste pipe replacement.

Groundwater depth in different parts of the neighbourhood

The surface of Pleskodāle is level, 9–10 m above sea level, and the highest point is in the centre of the neighbourhood by Pleskodāle cemetery. In the west and north-east groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, while in the centre and the south it is at a depth of 1.5–3 m.

In the eastern part, where an old tributary of the Mārupīte survives as a drainage ditch, there were 1–2 m of peat deposits, which were replaced with sand during development. It is the only place in the neighbourhood with difficult building conditions.

For the owner of a detached house this is a practical question when planning a new supply or a drain route through the garden. In the western part a trench can reach groundwater, and then the work takes more time. If you know where digging was done before and how deep the water was, write it in the form.

Connection to the street sewer and co-financing

The network extension under the Rīgas ūdens project “Water management development in Riga, stage 4” covered part of Pleskodāle. Under the Riga City Council binding regulations No. RD-23-207-sn, co-financing for a sewer connection depends on the length of the service pipe: up to 5 m €1,000, 5–20 m €1,500, over 20 m €2,000. It is granted after the connection is built, and it is not provided for a water supply connection.

Co-financing conditions as announced by Rīgas ūdens, checked 17.11.2026. Before the work, check the current conditions on the Rīgas ūdens website.

What residents ask

In our two-storey house the toilet is in the corridor, shared by everyone. Can a flat get its own?

Technically it usually can, if there is enough fall to the shared drain. Because the drain is shared by all the owners, first agree with the neighbours or the building manager. In the form, describe where the flat is and where the shared pipe runs.

In a one-and-a-half-storey house we want a bathroom upstairs under the roof. Where do we put the water heater?

The manufacturer Atlantic advises placing the water heater as close as possible to the draw-off points, in a room where the temperature is 4–35 °C. The upstairs partition must carry the weight of a filled water heater; above 100 l on a non-load-bearing wall Atlantic requires a stand. See water heater installation.

The house is in the western part of Pleskodāle. Does shallow groundwater get in the way of a new sewer connection?

In the western part groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, so a deep trench can reach the water. It does not rule out a service pipe, but the route and the depth must be planned in advance.

Does the co-financing cover a water supply connection too?

No. Under the conditions published by Rīgas ūdens, co-financing is provided for a sewer connection, but not for a water supply connection.
