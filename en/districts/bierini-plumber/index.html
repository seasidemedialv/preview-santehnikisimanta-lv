Plumber in Bieriņi, Riga - a shut-off valve map for a self-built house

A plumber in Bieriņi works in detached houses, many of which the owners built themselves after the Second World War: the land was divided into small plots for family homes, and building took place in conditions of general shortage.

In such a house the pipes may have been laid in stages over several decades, so before the work it helps to know where the incoming supply runs in the garden and where each shut-off valve is.

The meter turns, all taps closed

If the water meter turns although no tap in the house is open, first check two places that can let water through unnoticed: the toilet cistern and the water heater’s safety valve. If they are dry, close the incoming supply shut-off valve in the house and watch the meter. If it stops, the leak is in the house behind the valve. If it keeps turning, water is escaping in the incoming supply pipe between the meter and the house.

On Bieriņi’s oldest streets this pipe in the garden can have a history of its own. Sējas iela and Sīpeles iela by the Riga border appeared on the map in the early 20th century, when at Bieriņmuiža the swampy forests began to be drained and fields laid out. On former bog land a pipe can lie in wet ground, so the leak is located first and only then dug, rather than digging up the whole route.

Houses from the Sārtes iela era and extensions

In 1955 Sārtes iela joined Sējas iela and Sīpeles iela. In post-war Bieriņi it was permitted to build houses with a gable roof or so-called “ink bottles”, and some residents later fitted out rooms in extensions that were not originally intended for living.

If the bathroom or kitchen is in an extension, the drain can have a long horizontal stretch to the outlet, and the water supply can run in an outer wall that is not warm in winter. In such a house a shut-off valve map helps: a simple drawing of where the incoming supply valve, the valves to the extension and the water heaters are, and the outdoor tap. You can draw it yourself or together with the plumber when they first find the existing routes.

If a new pipe in the extension is hidden in a wall, the Swedish industry rules Säker Vatten require that pipes that cannot be inspected have no joints. Joints need an easily accessible service hatch, and it must not be placed in the shower or bath zone.

Cēres iela water main and the cistern filter

In 2026 Rīgas ūdens announced procurement RŪ-2026/109 for water main renewal on Cēres iela in the section from Pampāļu iela to Dauguļu iela, with a planned execution time of five months. Rīgas ūdens flushed the Bieriņi mains as planned in 2023.

After street works and connection to the new main, sediment can reach the taps in the house. Strainers and filters catch it, in the toilet cistern too: Geberit states that its fill valves have a water filter that protects the valve from dirt, and this filter can be replaced in a few steps. If the valve still does not work afterwards, replacing a flush mechanism is from €35.

What residents ask

Nobody knows where all the shut-off valves are in a house on Sārtes iela. Where do we start?

With the meter: from it follow the pipe to where it enters the house and on to each branch. Write every valve you find on the drawing with the room whose water it shuts off. If the route is not visible, a plumber visit with fault finding is from €40.

The bathroom is in an extension, and the water supply in the outer wall freezes in winter. What can be changed?

Move the pipe to the warm side of the wall or into an inner wall. Laying water supply pipe is from €10/m. If the new pipe is hidden, leave the joints where they can be reached through a hatch.

After the works on Cēres iela the toilet cistern fills very slowly. What could cause it?

One possible cause is a dirty fill valve filter that holds back sediment. In Geberit cisterns it can be replaced. If the cistern still fills slowly afterwards, describe in the form the type of cistern and the make, if visible.
