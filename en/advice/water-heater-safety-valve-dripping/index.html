Water heater safety valve dripping - normal or a fault

A water heater safety valve that drips into the funnel only while the heater is heating usually works correctly: water expands as it warms, and the excess has to get out.

If it also drips after heating, runs without stopping or never drips, the cause has to be found. In Imanta flats and in private houses in Kleisti or Beberbeķi it is the same assembly under the water heater, called the safety group.

The assembly under the water heater: what is in it

The safety group sits on the cold water supply, before the water enters the water heater. The manufacturer Caleffi describes its 5261 series group as one assembly containing a shut-off valve, a non-return valve with a test port, a safety valve, a funnel with an air gap and a handle for releasing water by hand. The non-return valve stops hot water from flowing back into the water supply.

Everything else follows from that. When the non-return valve is closed, the expanding warm water has no way back, and the pressure in the tank rises until the safety valve lets it out into the funnel. The manufacturer Stiebel Eltron (PSH Classic) says to flush the cold water pipe thoroughly before connecting the water heater, so that foreign particles do not get into the tank or the safety valve.

Drops, a stream or a dry funnel

Cause How to recognise it What to do
Expansion water while heating Drips only while the water heater is heating; when heating ends, the dripping stops That is how it should be. Stiebel Eltron (safety group ZH 1) writes that expansion water must drip into the funnel during heating
Dirt or limescale on the valve seat Drips also after heating, when no water is being drawn The Stiebel Eltron ZH 1 instructions allow the seat and seal to be cleaned with a damp cloth after unscrewing the top, without changing the opening pressure; for another model see its instructions
Worn internal parts of the group Leaks after years, even if the group was once fitted correctly Caleffi states that parts wear normally and can cause leaks; the group is repaired or replaced
There is a non-return valve, meter or pressure reducer at the incoming supply, but no expansion vessel Drips a lot at every heating, limescale builds up on the group Ariston (Andris Elite) requires an expansion vessel in this case, and Caleffi recommends it both against leaks and against limescale on the group
The non-return valve does not work The funnel stays dry during heating Stiebel Eltron advises checking the non-return valve; in a Caleffi group the non-return valve can be replaced

The expansion vessel on the cold water supply

The manufacturer Reflex places its Refix drinking water vessel on the cold water supply by the water heater, not on the hot water pipes, and requires a pressure reducer after the water meter. The vessel’s pre-charge pressure must be 0.2–1 bar lower than the reducer setting; if there is no reducer, 0.5 bar lower than the lowest pressure in the water supply. A wrong pre-charge wears the membrane faster.

The vessel size depends on the water heater’s volume. In the manufacturer Zilmet’s example, for a 300-litre system with a 3.3 bar pre-charge and a 6 bar safety valve, the calculation gives 18.7 l, so the nearest larger, 24 l vessel is chosen. Zilmet also warns that Hy-Pro vessels are not suitable if the water is additionally heated by a solid-fuel boiler or solar collectors.

The manufacturer Ariston supplies its Andris Elite 30 l model with a reducer set to 3.5 bar, and reminds that closing the tap on the supply reduces the flow, not the pressure. So throttling the supply tap does not stop the dripping.

What you can do yourself

  • Watch the funnel: does it drip only during heating, or also when the water heater is already idle.
  • Do not plug the funnel and do not close off the safety valve outlet; the expansion water has to get out.
  • Check that the outlet hose leads to the drain with an air gap and is not kinked.
  • If water runs without stopping, switch the water heater off at the fuse in the distribution board and close the shut-off valve in the safety group.
  • Clean the valve seat only if your model’s instructions allow it, and without abrasives or solvents.

Caleffi recommends checking the safety group every 6 months and servicing it every 12 months, referring to the standard EN 806-5. This is the manufacturer’s recommendation for its own assembly, not a Latvian rule.

In a flat or a private house: who deals with it

In a flat, the water heater and its safety group are installed behind the flat’s incoming supply valves, so it is usually the flat owner’s assembly, and the building manager need not be called. You need them only if the flat’s incoming valve does not close and the water has to be stopped at the riser. In a private house in Kleisti, Šampēteris or Bieriņi there is no building manager: according to Rīgas ūdens, the water pipe inside the house and on the way from the boundary of responsibility to the house is the customers' own responsibility. So the water heater is also the owner’s matter.

A plumber determines whether it is enough to clean the valve, replace the non-return valve or the whole safety group, or whether an expansion vessel has to be added. The price of a water heater repair is set after inspection. It depends on which part has to be replaced (the safety valve itself, the anode, the thermostat or the heating element), whether the water heater has to be taken off the wall and whether parts are still available for your model. A plumber visit with fault finding costs from €40.

If the water heater has to be replaced anyway, the new safety group and the supply pipes are part of the job water heater installation. For other water heater jobs see water heaters.

What residents ask

Can I put a plug on the safety valve so it does not drip onto the floor?

No. The expansion water has to get out, otherwise the pressure in the tank keeps rising. If the dripping is a nuisance, the solution is an outlet to the drain or an expansion vessel on the cold water supply.

The water heater is heating, but the funnel stays dry all the time. Is that fine?

Not necessarily. Stiebel Eltron states that expansion water must drip during heating, and if it does not, the non-return valve should be checked. The exception is a system with an expansion vessel that takes up this water.

How often should the safety group be checked?

The manufacturer Caleffi recommends a check every 6 months and servicing once every 12 months for its group. For another manufacturer the interval may differ, so see your model’s instructions.
