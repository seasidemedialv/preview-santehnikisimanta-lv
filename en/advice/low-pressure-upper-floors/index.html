Weak water pressure on upper floors - why and who to report to

Water pressure in a flat on the upper floors of Imanta’s nine-storey blocks is usually lower than at the bottom.

The water has to rise to the highest tap and also loses pressure to friction on the way. If only one tap is weak, the cause is usually in the tap itself or its supply. If every tap is weak and the neighbours have the same, it is a building matter, and the building manager is the first to report to.

Eight floors between the first and the ninth

A study of the 602 series commissioned by the Ministry of Economics states that these blocks are mostly nine-storey, the floor-to-floor height is 2.70 m and each floor of a section has 4 flats. The same study names Imanta among the Riga neighbourhoods where these blocks were built, and here the white nine-storey blocks stand next to the grey 464-series five-storey ones.

The manufacturer Wilo explains that pressure falls for two reasons: height (from the incoming supply to the highest tap) and friction in the pipes. In old pipes, corrosion and deposits increase the friction losses. In Imanta’s blocks, built mostly in 1967–1975, the ninth-floor tap receives both losses together.

For zone layouts in multi-storey buildings, the manufacturer Grundfos sets as a target that the pressure on the top floor of each zone does not fall below 1.5–2 bar and on the lowest floor does not exceed 4–4.5 bar. This is the manufacturer’s design target, not a Latvian standard, but it shows how large a difference between floors is to be expected.

One tap or the whole flat

Cause How to recognise it What to do
Limescale in a tap aerator or shower head Only one tap or shower is weak, the others run normally Unscrew the aerator or shower head and clean or descale it, as Stiebel Eltron advises
Clogged filter in an angle valve Weak flow at one sink after the water was shut off in the building In SCHELL valves with a filter, take it out with a coin or screwdriver and rinse it under running water
Shut-off valve at the meter not fully open Pressure fell after a renovation, a meter replacement or an emergency Open the incoming supply valve all the way, with the lever along the pipe
Overgrown or corroded galvanised pipe in the flat Weak throughout the flat, but normal for the neighbours on the same floor A plumber checks the pipes; an overgrown section is replaced
Low pressure at the building’s incoming supply or the building’s pump has stopped Weak on all upper floors, especially in the mornings and evenings Report to the building manager; this is the building’s equipment, not the flat’s

The Australian water company Unitywater reminds that pipes become overgrown over time and restrict the flow. In galvanised pipes, check whether corrosion is causing a blockage. A shut-off valve at the meter that is not fully open also reduces the pressure.

The bucket test at the tap nearest the meter

Measure before you report. Unitywater advises taking a 9-litre bucket and filling it from the tap nearest the meter. If it takes much longer than 30 seconds, the pressure may be too low. If the neighbours have no such problem, the cause is most likely in your own flat’s plumbing.

Note the time and the hour. The same company states that pressure depends on consumption and is usually lower at the morning and evening peaks. If on the ninth floor the bucket fills slowly in the morning but quickly at midday, that points to the building’s system, not to your tap.

Pumps in the cellar: building equipment, not the flat's

If the city network pressure does not reach the upper floors, the building may have a pressure booster system. The pump manufacturer KSB’s encyclopedia explains that such pumps are installed in a bypass at the main water pipe, and in the main pipe between the suction and pressure sides of the pumps there is a non-return valve.

Modern systems are run by variable-speed pumps. The Grundfos Hydro Multi-E maintains constant pressure by changing the pump speed and switching the needed number of pumps on or off, and after stopping for lack of water it restarts by itself in the factory setting. Wilo describes one pump running all the time while additional pumps join at peak times; a system chosen for average consumption is overloaded at the peak.

That is why a morning shower on the 9th floor can be weak even in a building with a pump. The system is chosen, adjusted and repaired at building level, so the building manager and the flat owners deal with it together, not a single flat.

What you can do yourself

  • Check that both incoming supply valves at the meters are fully open.
  • Unscrew and clean the aerator on the tap that runs weaker than the others.
  • Do the bucket test in the morning and at midday and write down the times.
  • Ask the neighbours on the same floor and the floor below whether they have the same.
  • Do not touch the meter seal and do not open the riser shaft with tools.

Building manager, Rīgas ūdens or plumber

The LV portal explains the order: if the pressure is weak throughout the building, first report to the building manager, in writing or by phone, and ask for a check. If the pressure does not change after the internal pipes have been checked, write to the water company, in Riga that is Rīgas ūdens, asking it to check the pressure at the building’s incoming supply.

What residents ask

Why does the shower on the ninth floor barely run in the morning but is fine at midday?

In the mornings many flats draw water at once, and the pressure usually falls at peak hours. The upper floors feel it first. If it happens every morning, report to the building manager and attach the bucket test times.

Do I have to write an application to the building manager, or is a phone call enough?

You can report to the building manager in writing or by phone. If nothing changes after the building’s internal pipes have been checked, write to the water company asking it to check the pressure at the building’s incoming supply.

The water runs weaker after a meter replacement. What should I check?

First the incoming supply valve: whether it is fully open and the lever is along the pipe. Then the aerators and the filters in the angle valves: Rīgas ūdens explains that salts and minerals deposited in pipes can come loose when the pressure changes.
