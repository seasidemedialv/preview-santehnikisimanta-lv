Moving plumbing outlets - while the walls are still open

Moving plumbing outlets is needed when, in a renovation, a sink, toilet, shower or washing machine goes to a new place.

In a panel flat in west Riga the distance is set by the soil stack: the further from it, the harder it is to get a fall. Outlets are made and tested before the walls are finished, because after tiling every mistake costs more.

When the old connection points no longer fit

  • In the new plan the toilet, sink or shower stands at a different wall than before.
  • A washing machine or dishwasher needs water and a drain where there never was any.
  • When the floor is opened, you see that a fixture is connected straight down into a horizontal pipe in the floor slab.
  • There is still a cast-iron branch pipe at the riser, and the new plastic pipe has to be joined to it.

If the place stays the same and only the fixture changes, the outlet usually does not have to be built anew: replacing the fixture and connecting it is enough.

Riser, floor slab and the building authority

Imanta’s multi-storey blocks were mostly built in 1967–1975, and the soil stack in them runs through all floors and links your flat with the neighbours above and below. The flat’s outlets connect to it, so choosing new places starts with the distance to the riser and with whether the way crosses the floor slab.

An explanation from the Riga Building Board in the LV portal says that whether a simplified procedure is enough for rebuilding a flat also depends on whether plumbing units are to be moved. Rebuilding common engineering systems needs a building design and a building permit. If a bathroom is joined to a room, the layout has to be drawn for the whole flat.

Explanation of the Riga Building Board, LV portal (2016). What exactly you need in your case, ask the Building Board; checked 17.11.2026.

How far from the riser you can go

What is moved What the rules or the manufacturer require What to watch
A toilet Säker Vatten: at a 200 mm floor slab the riser should be no further than 3.5 m from the toilet outlet; the toilet outlet can go straight to the riser. In practice the permitted length may be even shorter.
A sink, shower, washing machine Säker Vatten: up to 5 m; connect downstream with a branch pipe whose angle is no more than 45°. The branch angle makes backflow from the toilet harder.
A long branch with bends Pipelife (after Austrian ÖNORM B 2501): a connecting pipe longer than 4 m or with more than 3 bends needs separate ventilation. A shorter route is better than extra ventilation.
A water pipe in a wall Säker Vatten: concealed pipes that cannot be inspected must have no joints. Joints stay at a service hatch, not in the shower or bath zone.

Säker Vatten are Swedish industry rules, not a Latvian standard. They show well where leaks and blockages most often arise in a bathroom renovation.

Building the outlets step by step

  1. Plan on the floor Mark the riser position, each fixture’s outlet and the distances; check that every branch pipe has enough fall.
  2. Preparing the pipe In Pipelife’s instructions the pipe is cut square, burrs are removed and the end is chamfered at about 15°: for a 110 mm pipe the chamfer is 6 mm, for a 50 mm pipe 4 mm.
  3. Socket joints The pipe end is thinly coated with the manufacturer’s lubricant and pushed in all the way. Oil or grease must not be used as a lubricant.
  4. Transition to the old pipe A plastic pipe is joined to cast iron with a special transition piece; for example, the Pipelife 110 mm transition suits a cast-iron end with an outer diameter of 110–116 mm.
  5. Pipe in a wall If a drain pipe is built into a wall or plastered over, Pipelife advises wrapping it in insulation, for example a 4 mm PE tube, to reduce noise; the chase must let the pipe be laid without stress.
  6. Testing before finishing The water pipe is tested under pressure and the drain with water while all joints are still visible. The tiler starts only after that.

Moving a toilet to another wall

The toilet is the hardest fixture to move, because its outlet is the thickest and needs the largest fall. Säker Vatten warns that renovations often meet old solutions where a fixture is connected straight down into a horizontal pipe in the floor slab. Such a pipe is rerouted so that all connections are at no more than 45°, and this is planned before sawing or drilling concrete.

In a panel block the neighbour’s ceiling is under the floor slab, so chiselling into the floor without agreement is not a solution. It is often simpler to raise the toilet on a step or to choose a wall-hung toilet with a concealed frame, whose outlet goes into the wall.

A riser cleanout hatch is not tiled over tightly: Säker Vatten requires its lower edge to be at least 400 mm above the floor and a built-in hatch to keep a service opening.

What the cost of the outlets depends on

A new water or drain outlet costs from €55 per outlet if it is made during a renovation before the walls are finished. A new water pipe costs from €10/m, replacing a drain pipe in a flat from €30 per connection point. The total is set by the number of outlets, the distance to the riser and whether the old branch pipe has to be replaced. Chiselling and finishing are extra. Labour only, materials extra. The travel charge, if there is one, is named by the plumber on the call before starting the work.

What residents ask

Can a toilet be moved to the opposite wall of the bathroom?

It depends on the distance to the riser and on whether there is enough fall. At a 200 mm floor slab, Säker Vatten advises a toilet outlet no further than 3.5 m from the riser, though in practice it may be less. It is measured on site.

Does moving outlets have to be approved?

In the Riga Building Board’s explanation, moving plumbing units affects whether a simplified procedure is enough, and rebuilding common systems needs a building design. What exactly your flat needs, ask the Building Board and the building manager.

Why are water pipe joints not hidden in the wall?

A joint must stay reachable in case it ever needs tightening or replacing. Säker Vatten requires concealed pipes without joints and joints at a hatch outside the shower or bath zone.

What if there is still cast iron at the riser?

The new plastic pipe is joined with a special transition piece. If the cast-iron branch pipe has cracked, it is replaced; see waste pipe replacement.
