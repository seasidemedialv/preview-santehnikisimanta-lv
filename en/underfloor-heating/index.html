Water underfloor heating installation in a private house

Water underfloor heating installation in west Riga is a job for private houses: in Kleisti, Beberbeķi, Šampēteris, Bieriņi and Pleskodāle a house has its own heat source, and the floor loops can be connected to its own manifold.

In an Imanta panel flat this is not done from the building’s riser, and the reason is explained below.

When a water floor makes sense

  • The floor in the house is being renewed down to the base, and a new screed can be poured over the thermal insulation.
  • The house has a boiler or another heat source with its own circuit to which a manifold can be added.
  • The rooms have modern insulation, so the floor’s output can be enough without radiators.
  • One-and-a-half-storey standard-design houses are common in Pleskodāle; there each floor can get its own group of loops.

Why a panel flat's floor is not connected to the riser

In Imanta’s 464- and 602-series blocks the heating system is the common property of the flat owners: Rīgas siltums points this out about heat substations and internal heating systems. In an LV portal consultation about a flat where underfloor heating was connected to the building’s heating, the Ministry of Economics explained that rebuilding heating engineering networks without the proper documentation is classed as unauthorised construction.

There is also a technical reason. Post-war multi-flat buildings mostly have a one-pipe vertical system, where radiators receive heat in series. Floor heating needs a mixing unit that keeps the water cooler, but in a one-pipe riser the same water goes on after your flat to the neighbours. If you want a warm floor in a flat, talk to the building manager and a designer, not to a plumber alone.

Cement or anhydrite screed

Minimum thickness above the pipe (Uponor Tacker, 2.0 kN/m²) 45 mm 35 mm
Functional heat-up starts (EGGER, after DIN EN 1264-4) no earlier than after 21 days after 7 days
Drying heat-up starts (EGGER) no earlier than after 28 days after 14 days
Area without movement joints (Grant, after BS EN 1264-4) up to 40 m² for a semi-dry screed much larger areas are permitted

For example, in the British standards that Grant refers to, a screed on thermal insulation must be at least 65 mm thick, and floor heating must not be used to speed up the drying of the screed. Pouring the screed and the floor covering are other trades' work; the plumber is responsible for the pipes, the manifold and the pressure test.

From the manifold to the screed

  1. Output and spacing Grant states that the maximum output of floor heating is usually 70–100 W/m², and well-insulated buildings rarely need more than 70 W/m². For screed floors with a 16 mm pipe Grant advises a 200 mm spacing, where pipe use is 5.0 m/m²; at 150 mm it is 6.7 m/m², at 100 mm 10 m/m².
  2. Loop layout The flow pipe is led first to the coldest part of the room, under the windows and along the outer walls. Grant uses the serpentine (meander) pattern for large losses and the spiral pattern where an even floor temperature is needed.
  3. Laying the pipe Uponor allows its floor pipes up to 6 bar at 70 °C, but a bend must not be tighter than 8 × D by hand or 5 × D with a support; a tighter bend damages the pipe.
  4. Manifold and mixing unit Each loop is connected to the manifold. Grant states that under BS EN 1264 floor heating needs water temperature control: a mixing unit that mixes the heat source’s flow with the floor’s return.
  5. Test before the screed The loops are filled and pressure-tested while the pipes are still visible; only then is the screed poured.
  6. Functional heat-up EGGER describes a sequence: the flow is held at 25 °C for 3 days, then raised to the design maximum temperature, usually 45 °C, and held for 4 days without switching off at night. EGGER stresses that this does not yet mean the screed is dry enough for the covering, so drying time is needed.

Water underfloor heating and the house's radiators together

If floor heating is installed only on the ground floor or in the bathroom, but radiators stay on the upper floor, two temperatures run off one boiler: radiators with hotter water, the floor through the mixing unit with cooler. Grant points out that poorly insulated rooms may need additional heating in winter, so old radiators are not always removed. More about radiators on the page radiator replacement.

What makes up the cost of connecting a floor

There is no single starting price for this job, because houses differ. The price depends on the number and length of the loops and on whether the manifold and the pump group already exist. Pouring the floor and finishing are other trades' work. Labour only, materials extra. When you go over the house on the call, the plumber also says if getting there costs extra.

What residents ask

May water underfloor heating connected to the building's heating be installed in a bathroom in an Imanta flat?

The building’s heating system is common property, and the Ministry of Economics has called a rebuild without documentation unauthorised construction. Ask the building manager; it is not done without a design and approval.

How much pipe does a 20 m² room in a house in Bieriņi need?

At the 200 mm spacing Grant recommends, use is 5.0 m/m², so about 100 m of pipe, but it is set precisely by the room’s heat loss and the loop layout.

When can the floor be switched on after the screed?

EGGER, after DIN EN 1264-4, gives functional heat-up for a cement screed no earlier than after 21 days, for an anhydrite screed after 7 days. For a specific screed, its manufacturer’s instructions apply.
