Drain jetting - yard outlets in private houses in west Riga

Drain jetting with high-pressure water suits a yard outlet where a drain snake punches a hole through but grease, sand or roots stay on the walls.

In Kleisti, Beberbeķi, Šampēteris and the Imanta lines it is usually the pipe from the house to the chamber or the street network. Jetting is charged by the hour, and the first hour is at least €60.

When the drain snake is no longer enough

  • The outlet clogs repeatedly, and each time the drain snake opens it only for a while.
  • Root fibres or a grey layer of grease are visible in the chamber or the cleanout.
  • The pipe lies deeper than 2 m or is wider than 250 mm: TEPPFA states that a flexible rod usually suits only up to these limits.
  • After cleaning with a drain snake the water drains, but slowly, and you need to know what is left in the pipe.

New connections and old pipes in neighbourhood private houses

In phase 5 Rīgas ūdens built 9.88 km of sewer network in Beberbeķi, giving 150 households a chance to connect. In the Imanta lines, phase 6 built about 3.5 km of sewer for almost a hundred properties. The connection pipe from the street branch to the house belongs to the owner, so jetting it is the owner’s responsibility too.

The new connection pipe and the house’s old yard pipes can be of different materials. If the material is not known, it is established first, because the pressure and the nozzle depend on it. The ROTHENBERGER manual forbids cleaning asbestos with high-pressure water, and that applies to old asbestos-cement pipes as well.

Nozzle, pressure and pipe material

More pressure is not always better. In a TEPPFA comparison a 2.8 mm nozzle at 120 bar delivers about 5 times more energy than a 1 mm nozzle at 340 bar, and a larger volume of water carries the dirt to the chamber.

Situation What is used Limit
Soft grease and sludge deposits Jetting at lower pressure TEPPFA: 60 bar is usually enough
Thick layers on the pipe walls Jetting at 80–120 bar TEPPFA good practice: no more than 120 bar at the nozzle
A plastic pipe (PVC-U, PP, PE) in good condition A nozzle without chains or wires GAP guidelines: up to 120 bar, no longer than 10 seconds at a joint
The condition of the pipe is not known Moderate pressure, then a camera The WRc code (United Kingdom) advises no more than 130 bar
Roots in the pipe A root nozzle, for example ROTHENBERGER Root Ranger The manufacturer: in pipes wider than 150 mm, only with a fixed extension
An asbestos-cement pipe High-pressure water is not used The ROTHENBERGER manual’s ban

For penetrating nozzles ROTHENBERGER distinguishes three types with different jet layouts: 3 backward and 1 forward, 6 and 1, or only 6 backward. The backward jets pull the hose forward along the pipe and also clear the blockage and wash the dirt away.

How a yard outlet is jetted

  1. Entry to the pipe A chamber or cleanout is chosen to jet from. ROTHENBERGER requires a guard plate at the entry and a marking on the hose 1 m from the nozzle, because the nozzle in the pipe can turn through 180° and come back.
  2. Choosing the nozzle and pressure The nozzle and pressure are chosen by the pipe material and diameter, as in the table above.
  3. Moving towards the blockage The backward jets pull the hose forward and detach the deposits from the walls.
  4. Pulling back TEPPFA advises pulling the hose back at 6–12 m a minute, so the water carries the dirt to the chamber instead of leaving it in the pipe.
  5. Camera check TEPPFA advises inspecting the pipe with a video camera after jetting, to see the cause, for example a crack or a collapse, and to record it.

Drain camera inspection

After jetting the pipe is clean, and the camera shows its walls. The standard EN 13508-2 sets a coding system for describing the condition of external drains and chambers, starting from the place where wastewater leaves the building.

Each finding has a code, an extent and a position in the pipe. In the Flemish code of good practice BAA means deformation, BAB cracks, BAC a fracture or collapse, and BBA roots, with the extent given as a percentage. Such a record helps to decide whether jetting is enough or the section has to be replaced.

What the jetting price depends on

Jetting is charged by the hour, with at least one hour. The time is set by the pipe length, the thickness of the deposits, roots and how easily the chamber can be reached. If a drain snake has to be tried first, the row yard drain unblocking applies. The camera inspection price depends on the pipe length and access, on whether the pipe has to be jetted before the inspection, and on whether a recording is needed.

For example, if a yard outlet is jetted for two hours, that is at least €120. Whether travel has its own price, the plumber tells you before jetting begins. The page gives labour only, materials extra.

What residents ask

Will high-pressure water not damage a new plastic connection pipe?

In tests by the Danish Technological Institute, commissioned by TEPPFA, new and used PVC-U, PP and PE pipes withstood 50 jetting cycles at 120 bar with a 2.8 mm nozzle without damage. It is important to keep to the pressure limit and not to use nozzles with chains.

Will the roots not grow back after jetting?

They will. TEPPFA states that roots grow back, and only replacing the damaged section or removing the tree helps in the long term. Jetting frees the pipe for a time.

The house is old, and I do not know what the yard pipe is made of. What should I do?

Write it in the form. If the material is not known, a moderate pressure is chosen, and if asbestos cement is suspected, high-pressure water is not used.
