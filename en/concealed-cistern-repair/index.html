Most Imanta buildings were built in 1967–1975, so a cistern in the wall there is usually a later renovation job. A 1990s cistern has different parts from a TECE cistern after 2009. Most of the work is done through the opening of the button panel; replacing a flushing mechanism costs from €35.

Three cisterns in Imanta walls

An old GROHE cistern in the toilet of a five-storey 464-series block

In GROHE’s history the concealed cistern of 1981 held 9 litres, and the 1994 Rapid S with 6/9 litres was the first with a small service opening. If the cistern in the wall is older, it can have a larger flush and different parts, so the valve is looked up by model. On newer GROHE slim cisterns the button panel is connected pneumatically, and an extra flow regulator can be adjusted through the opening without taking anything apart.

A TECE cistern with an A 1 valve in a flat in a Zolitūde 119-series block

TECE cisterns have used the A 2 flush valve since 2009. If an old A 1 valve is replaced with an A 2, the manufacturer requires the activation lever to be replaced as well, and the old valve to be thrown away together with its seat and seal. So instead of one valve there are two assemblies.

A noisy Geberit cistern in a 602-series block

In a panel block the filling hiss is heard in neighbouring rooms too. Geberit states that its Type 383 fill valve halves the noise level compared with the earlier Type 380 (Unifill) and transmits less sound to neighbouring rooms. For old Geberit cisterns the manufacturer does not require replacing the whole cistern: the redesigned Type 208 flush valve is backward compatible.

Before any fill valve maintenance, the GROHE Rapid SL instructions require the water to the cistern to be shut off. After the panel is fitted the buttons must keep 1–2 mm of free play: Alcadrain states that without it the mechanism stays pressed and water runs.

Pressure on the top floor and the fill valve

If the cistern on the top floor of a nine-storey block fills slowly, it helps to know at what flow pressure the valve works. The TECE F 10 valve needs at least 500 hPa; the Geberit Duofix with a Sigma 12 cm cistern works at 0.1–10 bar and at 3 bar fills at 0.16 l/s. If the pressure is within range but the cistern fills slowly, check the filter first rather than replacing the valve.

The toilet leaks: the cistern or the ceramic

Alcadrain separates two cases. If the float has dropped, been fitted wrongly or set too high, water flows into the cistern continuously and goes through the overflow pipe into the toilet. A loose supply hose in the cistern can also hinder the float. If the cistern is empty but water runs from the rim of the toilet for a long time, that is a defect of the toilet. A weak trickle from the rim cavity for a few tens of minutes after flushing is still normal.

Alcadrain recommends servicing the cistern at least once a year: checking the flush volume, leaks and filling. If water appears on the floor or in the wall, shut off the water, see how to shut off water in a flat.

Two valves in one TECE cistern

For this job, what changes the amount most is whether the part fits the particular cistern without extra assemblies. Replacing a flushing mechanism from €35 includes replacing the inlet or outlet valve in a cistern, a concealed one too. A travel cost, where one applies, is stated on the call ahead of the repair. What residents ask

Can an old 9-litre SANIT cistern be converted to 6 litres?

If it is a SANIT cistern with the large service opening (type CC-120-L-FO, made in 1994–2010), yes. The SANIT INEO UPDATE kit reduces the flush from 9 to 6 litres, the volume can be adjusted within 4–6 and 3–4 litres, and a cable mechanism replaces the old lever.

A TECE cistern flushes 6 litres. Can it be set to 9?

On a TECE Uni cistern the large flush can be set to 4.5, 6, 7.5 or 9 litres, the small one is 3 litres, factory setting 6/3. On a TECE 8 cm cistern, for this the splash guard and the activation unit must be removed and the flush valve taken out.

In which cistern can the valves be taken out without tools?

In a Viega Prevista cistern the fill and flush valves are removed and fitted without tools. The small flush is set within 2–4 litres, the large within 3.5–7.5 litres, and the flow to the toilet is adapted with a ring in five steps.
